    STATUTORY UK SUPPORT

    UK Rights & Grants Hub

    Practical guidance on British support frameworks, including DWP Access to Work grants (£69,260), DSA, and the Equality Act 2010.

    Access to Work Grant (Up to £69,260/yr) DWP

    Funds workplace coaching, assistive software, and sensory equipment. No formal diagnosis required.

    Equality Act 2010 Section 20 Legal Right

    Statutory duty for UK employers and universities to provide reasonable adjustments based on daily impact.

    Personal Independence Payment (PIP) DWP / Non-Means Tested

    Financial support for extra daily living or mobility costs caused by long-term neurodivergent or sensory conditions (up to £194.60/wk total). No formal diagnosis required-assessed purely on functional daily impact.

    CIVIC LEGISLATION & INFRASTRUCTURE DIRECTIVE

    The Sanctuary Standard

    A campaign for mandatory low-demand sensory rooms in appropriately sized businesses and institutions, alongside sensory-safe protocols in public and civic buildings. Access to everyday life should not come at the cost of sensory safety.

    CAMPAIGN STATUS ACTIVE ADVOCACY
    TARGET BUSINESSES & INSTITUTIONS
    CORE GOAL MANDATORY SENSORY ROOMS
    PILLAR 01

    Acoustic & Visual Regulation

    Mandatory low-sensory rooms in appropriately sized businesses and institutions, alongside quiet zones, flicker-free lighting, and less relentless background music and announcements in essential services.

    PILLAR 02

    Literal, Zero-Subtext Signage

    Clear, literal wayfinding and direct assistance options so navigating a hospital, station, or council building doesn't burn through your entire executive function battery before you even arrive.

    PILLAR 03

    Non-Verbal & Low-Demand Access

    Written/text service channels, zero-eye-contact penalties, and physical retreat spaces to prevent sensory overload from turning into a public meltdown or shutdown.

    About AuDHMe

    Made from lived experience, shared with everyone.

    AuDHMe began with one person's need for clearer language and practical tools. It grows through the many ways neurodivergent people understand, adapt to, and move through the world.

    My part of the story

    Living at the AuDHD intersection

    I live with both autism and ADHD. Most days, the autistic need for routine, literal language and predictability collides with the ADHD need for novelty, movement and interest-led energy. Neither side is a flaw; they simply describe a brain navigating a world that was not always designed with it in mind.

    The masking, sensory overload, executive dysfunction and time blindness that often come with that experience are exhausting. AuDHMe grew from the wish for fewer explanations, clearer alternatives and tools that help me communicate before I run out of words.

    “I am not offering a finished definition of neurodivergence. I am sharing what I have learned so that others can recognise themselves, adapt what helps, and find language of their own.”

    The project started as something I needed personally. I continue it because no one should have to translate themselves into expensive jargon or apologise for needing access. Whether you are autistic, ADHD, AuDHD, dyslexic, dyspraxic, living with OCD, or carrying another kind of difference, you belong here without having to prove it.

    A shared direction

    Not a hierarchy. A useful starting point.

    01

    Listen before explaining

    People are experts in their own experience, even when their needs or words change.

    02

    Keep access practical

    Good support should reduce effort, clarify choices and make everyday life more possible.

    03

    Leave room for difference

    There is no single way to be neurodivergent, and no need to arrive fully understood.
